The record

One checked record per word.

A wrong word is this app’s false fact. So a pronunciation, a meaning, an origin and a date are each treated as a claim, and each one in a record is held to the page it came from.

Printed by Cambridge and Dictionary.com for US English, and by Cambridge and Dictionary.com for UK English.

The tiles

The headword cut into syllables, as a dictionary cuts it. When every printed way stresses the same syllable, that tile is lit. When the ways differ, none is lit until you tap a way, and the capitals in each respelling carry the stress.

The respelling

Our own spelling of the sound: syllables joined by hyphens, the stressed one in capitals. It follows one key, below.

The IPA

For UK and for US English, one of the dictionaries’ own transcriptions, whole. Nobody types IPA from memory, and where no dictionary prints a way whole in IPA the respelling stands alone.

C, L, D, A

Cambridge, Longman, Dictionary.com, American Heritage. A filled letter means that dictionary prints this way for that English. Under it the same thing is said in words: “Printed by…”.

One way, or two

Two ways are two ways when a listener would hear two words: a different stressed syllable, a different vowel in it, a sound added or dropped. The same way in two accents is one way.

No way is ranked. The order is the order most dictionaries give.

What a record rests on

A way to say a word, a meaning and an origin rest on four dictionaries.

  • CCambridgeIPA, meaning, examples. UK and US English.
  • LLongmanIPA, syllable division, meaning, origin with a century. UK and US English.
  • DDictionary.comIPA, meaning, origin with first-recorded dates, usage notes. US English, and UK English from another dictionary’s entries.
  • AAmerican HeritageIts own respelling, syllable division, meaning, origin, usage notes. US English.

Wiktionary stands beside them: it is the only source here for Englishes beyond the UK and the US, and it is quoted with its own label. A pronunciation that only Wiktionary records does not make a way; it goes in the note, with Wiktionary named.

Dictionary.com’s page also shows a plain-language paragraph from another site. A record may quote it as a memory aid. It counts toward no rule that asks for two sources, it is never one of the four in a count, and no line shown to a person rests on it alone.

A dictionary’s own marks are part of what it prints, and a record never flattens them: a letter Cambridge raises stays raised, and a letter Longman sets in italics, for a sound that may be left out, is written in brackets.

The meanings and origins are our own writing. No dictionary’s definition, example or note is copied.

The dictionaries are named because their pages were read. None of them has given VocabExpand consent or a licence.

The rules a record keeps

  1. Every way the four dictionaries record is in the record. Each transcription printed under the headword is in a way, or set aside with a reason.
  2. “One way” is a count, and the record makes it.
  3. Where a dictionary carries a usage note, the record says what is disputed and who says so, and takes no side the dictionaries do not take. A label such as “nonstandard” is reported as the label it is.
  4. A myth is never shown alone. A false origin is told only when a source calls it false, and the true origin, or “nobody knows”, comes in the same breath.
  5. If the dictionaries disagree on a fact, the record says the less: the century they share, the language they share.
  6. A rhyme is a claim about two words, so both words are checked against their dictionary pages.
  7. Nothing about people without a source. The record says what the spelling suggests, never what “most people” say.
  8. No word that is not a word. A headword is in at least one of the four dictionaries.

How a record is checked

  1. A script finds each transcription on the dictionary page it names, with the English that page gives it.
  2. The same script finds every quoted fact on its page, word for word, and fails a meaning that runs six words with any source.
  3. Then a second reader that did not write the record reads it against the rules.
  4. A record that fails does not ship.

The writer and the second reader are language models. The script is a script.

If a shipped word is found false it is withdrawn: a reviewed pack version carries the withdrawal, and once it reaches a phone the word leaves every screen at the app’s next run or background refresh.

What a record does not say

It does not say how people speak. A count is of dictionaries, and “only” is never used of speakers. An English that no source records is not called wrong, and it is not called the same as the UK’s: the record says nothing.
